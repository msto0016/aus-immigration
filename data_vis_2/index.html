Australia's Need for Immigration

Australia is ageing faster than ever

Australia’s population is undergoing a significant demographic transformation. People are living longer than at any point in our history, while birth rates continue to fall. As shown in the population pyramids, the age structure that once resembled a classic pyramid, with many children, fewer adults, and very few older people, has reshaped into a more rectangular, top‑heavy form.

More than 22% of Australians are now aged 65 and over, and this number is projected to exceed 4.5 million in the coming decades. This shift places growing pressure on systems that depend on a balanced age mix, including healthcare, aged care, the workforce, and housing.

The workforce is shrinking relative to retirees

As the population ages, the number of Australians in the labour force is struggling to keep pace with the rising number of retirees. Although employment has increased, retirees have grown faster, pushing the support ratio downward.

With more people leaving the workforce and fewer entering it, Australia faces labour and skills shortages, reduced economic dynamism, and increased fiscal pressure. Older Australians typically require more healthcare and support services, meaning demand is rising just as the pool of workers who fund and staff these systems is shrinking.

Births alone cannot sustain population growth

Australia’s fertility rate has been declining for decades. Fertility has shifted, once concentrated among women aged 20-29, is now highest among those aged 30-39. This reflects people are choosing to have children later in life, often prioritising higher education or careers.

Many are also choosing to have smaller families due to cost‑of‑living pressures, housing shortages and affordability, climate concerns, and shifting cultural norms. Greater accessibility to contraception has also influenced birth rates.

Fertility has remained below replacement level since the 1970s and reached a record low during COVID‑19, suggesting births alone are not capable of maintaining population stability.

Immigration is now the primary driver of population growth

Migration has long shaped Australia’s population. Today, 8.8 million people, or 32% of the population, were born overseas.

In recent decades, net overseas migration (NOM) has become the dominant contributor to population growth, while natural increase (births minus deaths) shows a downward trend.

The COVID‑19 border closures were a stark reminder of the influence of migration on population growth. When migration was halted, population growth fell sharply, and labour shortages intensified. After borders reopened in 2022, NOM surged as deferred arrivals caught up.

Where are people migrating from?

Australia’s migrant intake is diverse and continually evolving. Of the 8.83 million overseas born residents, 971,020 were born in India, a number that has grown rapidly over the past four years.
